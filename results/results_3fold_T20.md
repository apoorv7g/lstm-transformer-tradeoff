Metric notes (all on the held-out test fold):

- **AUROC**: primary metric. 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect.
- **Sens@Spec90**: share of Abnormal clips caught while only 10% of Normal clips are flagged (read off the ROC curve).
- **High-sensitivity cut-off**: chosen on the inner validation set to catch 95% of Abnormal clips, then applied unchanged to test. **Missed** = Abnormal clips it still misses per test fold.
- **MACs**: analytic multiply-accumulates per clip for the temporal model and head; the shared MobileNetV2 backbone is not included.

## Findings

These are the findings of this 42-run batch. All numbers are means over the 6 test folds
(3 folds x 2 seeds) at T = 20 unless stated otherwise. The tables further down hold the detail.

### 1. Ranking quality (AUROC): every model is equally good

- AUROC ranges only from 0.893 (`pool_max`) to 0.909 (`hybrid_attn`). The spread between models
  (0.016) is about the size of the fold-to-fold spread of a single model (sd 0.009 to 0.022).
- No model differs significantly from `lstm_base` on the corrected resampled t-test (all p >= 0.26).
- **Main hypothesis H0-acc (Transformer AUROC = LSTM AUROC): supported.** `transformer_base` minus
  `lstm_base` is -0.0014 AUROC; the 90% CI [-0.019, +0.016] lies inside the pre-registered
  equivalence margin of +/- 0.03, so the two are statistically equivalent at T = 20.
- `hybrid_attn` (+0.0018) and the no-temporal `pool_mean` (-0.0072) are also equivalent to
  `lstm_base`. `hybrid_base`, `hybrid_lite` and `pool_max` are not shown equivalent, but only
  because their CIs are wider than the margin; none is significantly worse.
- An earlier, deleted 5-fold batch ranked `hybrid_lite` first; here it is mid-table. The ranking
  between models is not stable across fold splits, which confirms that the differences are noise.

### 2. Frame order adds little: the no-temporal baseline nearly matches

- `pool_mean` ignores frame order completely (average of the 20 frame embeddings, then the shared
  head). It reaches 0.900 AUROC with 82,049 parameters and 0.08 M MACs, against 0.907 for
  `lstm_base` with 1.78 M parameters and 35.4 M MACs (about 430 times the compute).
- The one place where temporal models lean ahead is **sensitivity at 90% specificity**:
  `hybrid_attn` 0.760 and `lstm_base` 0.749, against `pool_mean` 0.665 and `pool_max` 0.639. The
  gap (`pool_mean` -0.084 vs `lstm_base`) is not significant with 6 folds (p = 0.11), so it is a
  lead to test with more seeds, not a finding.
- `pool_max` (per-feature maximum over frames) is slightly worse than `pool_mean` on every
  threshold-free metric; keeping the strongest single-frame response does not help on its own.

### 3. Accuracy, precision and recall depend on the cut-off, not only on the model

- **Plain accuracy is 0.82 to 0.85 for every model at the 0.5 and Youden cut-offs.** Answering
  Normal for every clip already scores 0.736, so accuracy hides most of the differences and is not
  used to decide.
- **At the default cut-off 0.5 the models make very different trade-offs:**
  - `lstm_base` is the most cautious: precision 0.755, recall 0.623, specificity 0.919. It misses
    29 of 77 Abnormal clips per fold.
  - `pool_mean` is the most eager: precision 0.641, recall 0.786, specificity 0.840 (16.5 missed).
  - `transformer_base` sits between them: precision 0.705, recall 0.712 (22 missed).
  - `hybrid_attn` has the highest accuracy at 0.5 (0.849) and the highest MCC (0.615).

  These differences come from where each model happens to put its probabilities relative to 0.5,
  not from how well it separates the classes (AUROC is tied).
- **At the Youden cut-off (chosen on validation) the models even out:** recall 0.78 to 0.82,
  specificity 0.83 to 0.87, precision 0.63 to 0.70, F1 (Abnormal) 0.70 to 0.74, MCC 0.59 to 0.65.
  `lstm_base` is best on accuracy (0.852), precision (0.704), F1 (0.741) and MCC (0.645) here, but
  all within one standard deviation of the others. This is the fairest like-for-like comparison of
  the threshold metrics.
- Report the validation-chosen cut-offs, not 0.5. The 0.5 numbers are kept for completeness.

### 4. Screening view: not missing Abnormal clips

- With the cut-off chosen on validation to catch 95% of Abnormal clips, **test recall is 0.87 to
  0.92**: on average 6 to 10 of the 77 Abnormal clips per fold are still missed. The validation
  target of 95% is not fully reached on test, as expected from a validation set with only 23
  Abnormal clips.
- The price is false alarms: specificity falls to 0.66 to 0.76 and **precision to 0.50 to 0.58**,
  so roughly every second clip flagged Abnormal is actually Normal (about 52 to 74 false alarms
  per fold, out of 214 Normal clips).
- **NPV is about 0.95 for every model**: a clip the model clears is Normal 95% of the time. This is
  the number that matters for a screening tool that rules clips out.
- The pooling baselines miss the fewest clips at this cut-off (`pool_max` 6.0, `pool_mean` 7.0)
  but raise the most false alarms (`pool_max` 73.5, `pool_mean` 65.2). The temporal models miss a
  few more (8.7 to 10.2) with about 52 to 58 false alarms. None of these differences is clearly
  beyond fold noise.
- No temporal model clearly improves the miss rate. Catching substantially more Abnormal clips
  without many more false alarms most likely needs better frame features, not a different
  temporal block.

### 5. Calibration: probabilities are too low for Abnormal clips

- Abnormal test clips receive a mean predicted probability of only 0.62 to 0.70; Normal clips 0.09
  to 0.26. The models rank well but their probabilities are not calibrated, which is another
  reason the 0.5 cut-off misbehaves.
- `pool_mean` has the best Brier score (0.118) and log loss (0.373); the larger temporal models are
  more over-confident (log loss 0.50 to 0.59). If probabilities are to be shown to a clinician,
  they should be recalibrated on validation data first (for example Platt scaling).

### 6. Training cost

- Mean fit time per fold on 4 CPU threads: pools 9 s, hybrids 23 to 27 s, `transformer_base` 35 s,
  `lstm_base` 38 s. Early stopping fires after 28 to 43 epochs on average; the pools run the most
  epochs but are by far the fastest.

### 7. Compute: CPU latency (measured, 48 benchmark jobs, 0 failed)

- **Batch-1 latency on 1 thread** (one clip at a time, the realistic screening case): `lstm_base`
  3.54 ms, `hybrid_base` 2.12 ms, `transformer_base` 1.36 ms, `hybrid_attn` 1.11 ms, `hybrid_lite`
  0.92 ms, `pool_mean` 0.22 ms, `pool_max` 0.25 ms.
- **H0-cpu (Transformer batch-1 latency <= LSTM's): not rejected; the data go the same way.**
  `transformer_base` is 2.6 times faster than `lstm_base` at batch 1 on 1 thread (1.36 vs 3.54 ms)
  and 2.9 times faster on 8 threads (2.02 vs 5.82 ms), consistently in all 3 sessions. The LSTM has
  to process the 20 frames one after another; attention handles them in parallel.
- **At batch 32 the gap closes.** On 1 thread the Transformer is still faster (23.3 vs 29.0 ms per
  batch), but on 8 threads the two are equal (19.1 vs 18.9 ms). For bulk offline scoring the
  choice does not matter; for one-clip-at-a-time use the Transformer wins.
- **More threads make single clips slower.** At batch 1, every temporal model is 1.4 to 1.8 times
  slower on 8 threads than on 1 (for example `lstm_base` 3.54 to 5.82 ms): the work per call is too
  small to share, so thread coordination dominates. Run the temporal model on 1 thread.
- `hybrid_attn` is 3.2 times faster than `lstm_base` and `hybrid_lite` 3.9 times; the pools are
  about 15 times faster. Latency follows MACs in order, but not in proportion: the 430-fold MAC gap
  between `pool_mean` and `lstm_base` becomes a 16-fold latency gap, because fixed per-call
  overhead dominates such small models.
- Memory is flat: peak process memory 330 to 410 MB for every model, almost all of it TensorFlow
  itself.

### 8. Compute in context: the shared front end dominates

- Every model needs the same front end per clip: decoding the video and sampling 20 frames (about
  145 ms), preprocessing (about 4 ms) and MobileNetV2 on 20 frames (about 312 ms on 1 thread,
  125 ms on 8 threads).
- **End to end, the temporal model is under 1% of the time on 1 thread.** A clip takes about
  465 ms with `lstm_base` and about 462 ms with `transformer_base`; the 2 ms difference is 0.5% of
  the total. On 8 threads (backbone faster, temporal part slower) `lstm_base` is still only about
  2% of the total.
- So the LSTM vs Transformer choice is decisive only for the temporal step in isolation (2.6 times).
  For the full pipeline it is negligible next to the frozen CNN and video decoding; reducing T or
  using a lighter backbone would save far more.

### 9. What this means for the study

- **H0-acc:** the Transformer and LSTM are equivalent in AUROC (within +/- 0.03).
- **H0-cpu:** the Transformer is faster at batch 1 (2.6 times for the temporal step), so the null
  that it is no slower holds; at batch 32 on 8 threads they tie.
- **Overall:** at equal accuracy, the Transformer is the better temporal model for single-clip CPU
  inference, but in a full pipeline the difference shrinks to under 1% of the latency.
- **Best overall model in this batch: `hybrid_attn`.** It ties for the best AUROC (0.909), has the
  best sensitivity at 90% specificity (0.760) and the best accuracy and MCC at 0.5, at 1.11 ms per
  clip (3.2 times faster than `lstm_base`) and 8.6 M MACs.
- **Cheapest acceptable model: `pool_mean`.** About 0.007 AUROC below `lstm_base` (equivalent within
  the margin) at 0.22 ms per clip (16 times faster), but about 8 points lower on sensitivity at 90%
  specificity.

### 10. Limits of this batch

- 6 paired folds give wide confidence intervals; a third seed would narrow them.
- Only T = 20 was run; the effect of the number of frames (H0-T) is untested.
- There is no patient ID, so retakes of the same child may sit in different folds. This affects
  all models alike, so the comparison stays fair, but absolute scores may be optimistic.
- Compute was measured at T = 20 only, on one laptop; the end-to-end figures add separately measured
  parts (decode, preprocessing, backbone, temporal model) rather than timing one fused pipeline.

## Threshold metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and more (T = 20)

These depend on the cut-off that turns a probability into a Normal / Abnormal decision, so they are given at three cut-offs. Abnormal is the positive class: **precision** = of the clips flagged Abnormal, how many are; **recall** = sensitivity = of the Abnormal clips, how many are flagged; **specificity** = of the Normal clips, how many are cleared; **NPV** = of the clips cleared, how many are truly Normal; **MCC** = Matthews correlation (-1 to 1, robust to imbalance). Plain accuracy is reported but never used to decide. TP / FP / TN / FN are mean counts per test fold.
